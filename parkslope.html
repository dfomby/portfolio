Park Slope Brochure

Trifold Brochure

The Park Slope Brochure is a tri-fold design that highlights the neighborhood’s landmarks, history, notable celebrity connections, along with my personal relationship to Park Slope. It is intended to engage New York residents and visitors who may be interested in exploring the area.

My Role: Graphic Designer

Year: 2026

Tools: InDesign, Photoshop

Project: Concept/Hypothetical Project

Final Designs

The Challenge

Park Slope is a scenic neighborhood located in Brooklyn, NY. The neighborhood is known for its brownstone home history, and today the neighborhood can be described as eclectic and family friendly.

There are many elements and places located in Park Slope that needs to be conveyed within the brochure. The main challenge is to not only make the brochure appealing for potential visitors, but to make all of its history and elements easy to consume and follow.

The problem I need to solve: How can the brochure properly appeal to potential visitors, while portraying the eclectic nature of Park Slope?

Research and Ideation

The brochure's purpose:
The brochure needs to successfully encapsulate the atmosphere and architecture of Park Slope, while also highlighting its main locations.

Key Insights

Insight 1: Historical Architecture

Historically known for its brownstone architecture, especially its brownstone homes.

Insight 2: Locations and stores

Park Slope's most known locations are Grand Army Plaza, 7th Avenue, 5th Avenue, and its neighboring park, Prospect Park. The neighborhood also contains various different boutiques, bars and restaurants.

Target Audience

New York Residents and/or visitors who want to explore Park Slope's main locations, and learn about its history.

Concept Development

Mood-Board

To begin planning out the design, color scheme and typography for the brochure, I created a mood-board dedicated to these elements.

Drafting the Brochure

First Draft

While the first draft encapsulates the architecture of the brochure, both the color scheme and design of the brochure's pages doesn't properly portray Park Slope as a whole, especially for its eclectic atmosphere and locations. This draft also isn't very appealing to its targeted audience.

Final Draft

By revamping the entire design for the brochure, it now portrays the vibrant and calming aesthetics of Park Slope, while also highlighting its locations and architecture. The new design for the inside pages also has a playful effect that guides the reader throughout the page, grabbing their attention effectively.
